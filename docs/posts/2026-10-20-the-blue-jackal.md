# The Indigo Coat: How Borrowed Authority Crumbles When Instinct Takes Over

![Vedic Sage Instructing Disciple on the Psychology of the Blue Jackal](../assets/images/posts/2026-10-20-the-blue-jackal.jpg)

The late morning sunlight drifted through the canopy of the forest hermitage, tracing intricate webs of gold upon the earthen courtyard. Beneath the sprawling limbs of an ancient banyan tree, the air carried the sharp fragrance of dry leaves and sacred samidha wood. Varun, a seasoned engineering director from the capital's mercantile software guild, sat upon a coarse grass mat with his shoulders hunched and his hands clenched tightly against his knees. The tight, bitter lines around his mouth betrayed the exhaustion of someone who had watched an unqualified peer seize absolute command through theatrical deception.

Across from him sat the venerable Rishi, still and observant as deep water. His silver beard flowed over an unstitched cotton dhoti, his matted hair was gathered in a disciplined Vedic *rishi jata* topknot, and beads of dark rudraksha encircled his wrists. A faint mark of sacred ash touched his forehead. He observed the tension in Varun's breathing before breaking the silence.

"You carry the silent rage of a craftsman forced to bow to an idol carved from hollow clay, Varun," the Rishi said softly.

Varun let out a long, shuddering breath. "Revered Sage, three quarters ago, our executive guild hired a man named Devraj to lead our core distributed infrastructure. He arrived from an acquired enterprise carrying the aura of a high-profile luminary. He possessed none of the architectural competence required to maintain our transaction pipelines, yet he spoke with an extraordinary vocabulary of foreign strategic buzzwords. He wore the immaculate veneer of an industry visionary, and our senior executives were completely mesmerized."

Varun leaned forward, his voice tightening. "Devraj immediately exiled every veteran architect who questioned his technical feasibility, excluding us from roadmap discussions while surrounding himself with junior yes-men. He demanded unquestioned subservience from senior engineers, claiming his authority came directly from the founder's confidential mandate. We bowed our heads, swallowed our pride, and labored late into the night to prevent his catastrophic system redesigns from collapsing. Then, during yesterday's critical board outage review, a simple operational question stripped his composure away. He panicked, threw a junior developer under the bus, and shrieked incoherently about personal disloyalty before the entire steering committee. The mask fell, yet my team remains demoralized and broken. How does a leader build absolute dominion upon sheer illusion, and how do we protect our craft when false majesty reigns?"

## The Jackal in the Indigo Vat

The Rishi smiled gently, picking up a fallen banyan leaf and turning it between his weathered fingers.

"Your grief is sharp, Varun, but the spectacle you witnessed is ancient. Pandit Vishnu Sharma recorded this very pattern thousands of years ago in the *Panchatantra*, in the foundational book of *Mitra-Bheda*, through the famous tale of Chandarava—the Blue Jackal."

"In a dense jungle there lived an ordinary jackal named Chandarava. Thin, hungry, and driven by an empty stomach, he prowled into the outskirts of a human city in search of food. The fierce city dogs caught his scent and chased him relentlessly through the narrow alleys, snapping at his heels and tearing at his flesh. In desperate panic, fleeing for his life, Chandarava leapt over a low mud wall and plunged headlong into a massive stone vat filled with deep blue indigo dye at a dyer's house."

"When the barking hounds lost his trail and moved away, Chandarava dragged himself out of the vat. When he looked down at his paws and belly, he was astonished. From the tip of his snout to the tail of his coat, the indelible dye had soaked through his fur, turning him a brilliant, otherworldly shade of deep indigo. When he returned to the forest, the wild creatures looked upon him with sheer terror. The mighty lions, the swift tigers, the panthers, wolves, and wild elephants had never beheld such a creature. Believing him to be a terrible new monster endowed with supernatural power, the beasts turned and fled in panic."

"Seeing the apex predators cower before him," the Rishi continued, "Chandarava's cunning awakened. He called out in a loud, commanding voice: *'O beasts of the forest! Why do you run in fear? Lord Brahma himself saw that this kingdom lacked a sovereign protector, and with his own hands he anointed me Kakudruma, the monarch of all four-legged beings. Lay down your dread and dwell peacefully under my supreme command!'*"

"The wild beasts rejoiced, blinded by his startling coat. They prostrated themselves before him and surrendered their sovereignty. Chandarava assigned royal offices with grandiose ceremony: he appointed the majestic lion as his prime minister, the fierce tiger as his master of the royal bedchamber, the leopard as keeper of the gate, and the elephant as his personal guard. The finest game hunted by the predators was laid at his feet."

"Yet Chandarava knew a secret fear: those of his own kind possessed the memory of his true origins. To protect his grand illusion, the Blue Jackal issued an immediate royal decree expelling every ordinary jackal from the forest, forbidding them from ever entering his royal presence under penalty of death. For a time, he reigned without dispute, puffed up with borrowed grandeur."

"Then came an evening when the monsoon mist gathered over the hills. Sitting upon his high rock among the reverent lions and tigers, Chandarava heard a distant pack of common jackals howling in unison under the rising moon. The sound pierced through his borrowed majesty. The primal instinct of his blood surged through his veins. His throat swelled, his whiskers twitched, and despite all his desperate effort to maintain royal silence, he could not suppress the nature stamped into his marrow. Throwing back his head, he let out a piercing, ragged howl: *'Phee-ooh! Phee-ooh!'*"

"The lion and the tiger stiffened. They looked down at the howling creature beside them, and the truth struck like lightning. *'This is no divine emissary,'* the lion roared. *'It is merely a common jackal dressed in stolen blue dye!'* Before Chandarava could take a single step toward the bushes, the outraged predators fell upon him and tore him to shreds."

## The Corporate Indigo Vat: How Borrowed Prestige Fools the Herd

The Rishi set the banyan leaf upon the mat. "The dye vat changes the coat, Varun, but it leaves the blood unaltered. In human enterprises, the indigo vat takes modern forms."

**The Accidental Veneer.** A mediocre professional lands in a celebrated brand, a windfall project, or acquires an executive sponsor through proximity rather than craft. They absorb the external vocabulary of strategic leadership without ever mastering the fundamental disciplines of execution. When colleagues look at their pedigree, they see an exotic beast and mistake borrowed prestige for genuine wisdom.
> *"He was on the core team during the unicorn IPO; whatever methodology he brings must be followed without question."*

**The Exile of the Truth-Tellers.** Like Chandarava expelling the jackal pack, an impostor executive instinctively banishes the practitioners who recognize their technical shallowness. They cannot afford to keep veteran domain experts in the council room, because an authentic peer sees right through the dye. They replace critical thinkers with sycophants who worship the coat and never inspect the teeth.
> *"The legacy engineering leads are too set in their old ways; let us reassign them to maintenance so they do not obstruct our transformation."*

**The Primal Howl Under Crisis.** A veneer of authority holds only while conditions are scripted and the predators are compliant. But real corporate crises generate unexpected friction. When a major service collapses, an urgent audit arrives, or an investor asks an unscripted question, the borrowed vocabulary evaporates. The leader reverts instantly to their primitive instincts: finger-pointing, shrill defensiveness, or terrified silence.
> *"This catastrophic failure is entirely the fault of the on-call engineer who misapplied my conceptual vision!"*

**The Retribution of the Competent.** When the true nature slips out, the disillusionment is swift and unsparing. The senior contributors who did the actual labor realize they surrendered their autonomy to an illusion. Once respect vanishes, authority cannot be restored by decree. The organization strips the pretender of influence with the same fierce momentum with which it once elevated them.

## Four Disciplines for Navigating Borrowed Authority

Varun sat silently for a moment, the tension in his shoulders beginning to loosen. "Revered Sage, if an impostor leader holds administrative rank over our guild, how do we endure their reign without sacrificing our team or our dignity?"

The Rishi leaned forward and spoke with measured authority.

### 1. Document Commitments in Objective Ledgers
Do not argue with an impostor's credentials in open debate; measure their directives against written records. When a leader proposes ambitious changes with vague claims, translate every promise into an explicit, written architecture specification with measurable latency, cost, and reliability metrics. Send a clear summary email after every design session stating the agreed scope, dependencies, and owners. False authority thrives in verbal ambiguity; it dissolves when pinned to concrete deliverables.

### 2. Preserve Lateral Alliances Across the Guild
An insecure leader will always attempt to isolate domain veterans from one another to prevent mutual verification. Counter this tactic by establishing regular, informal technical review forums with peer architects and engineering leads across adjacent teams. Share architectural patterns, cross-team dependencies, and system health reports openly. When the veteran practitioners remain united in shared reality, an isolated pretender cannot divide your ranks or manufacture false narratives of failure.

### 3. Maintain Operational Sovereignty
Never surrender custody of your core production safeguards, deployment checklists, or rollback mechanisms to executive theatre. If an unqualified leader orders a reckless architectural shortcut, resist passive-aggressive compliance. State the operational hazards calmly in written risk registers, outline the recovery costs, and require explicit written sign-off before dangerous changes enter the release pipeline. When the inevitable failure occurs, your team will stand protected by clear evidence of professional diligence.

### 4. Wait for the Inevitable Howl
Never launch a premature rebellion against a leader who still enjoys the uncritical favor of the executive board. A direct attack on their credentials will only paint you as insubordinate and rally the board to their defense. Focus your energy on maintaining pristine craft and protecting your junior engineers. Continue doing your duty with calm detachment. Under sustained operational pressure, the pretender's own nature will inevitably trigger their exposure far more decisively than any accusation you could ever level.

**Source:** Panchatantra, Book 1 (Mitra-bheda), "The Blue Jackal" (Chandarava Katha)

Varun bowed low before the sage, the burning grievance in his chest replaced by quiet, strategic patience. He gathered his notebooks, rose with steady balance, and stepped onto the sunlit path toward the city, ready to anchor his team in quiet competence while the winds of time tested the dye.

> *"A bath in indigo may dazzle the forest for a season, but when the distant pack cries out, the jackal must howl."*
